# Teaching and learning with AI

> Sample syllabus statements, assignment design tips, and boundaries for using Copilot in teaching.

Faculty decide how AI may be used in their courses within the organization's academic policies. Students need that decision stated clearly in every course. The statements below are starting points to adapt; they are not organization-mandated language.

## Sample syllabus statements

### Option 1: AI not permitted

"In this course, generative AI tools such as Copilot may not be used to complete graded work, because the course goals depend on you developing these skills yourself. If you are unsure whether a tool counts, ask me before using it."

### Option 2: AI permitted for specific tasks, with disclosure

"You may use Copilot for brainstorming, outlining, and checking grammar. You may not use it to write graded text or solve graded problems. Add a short note to each assignment describing how you used AI and what you changed. You are responsible for the accuracy of everything you submit."

### Option 3: AI encouraged and integrated

"We will use Copilot as a learning tool in this course. Some assignments ask you to use it and to evaluate its output critically. Cite AI assistance as described in each assignment. An equivalent non-AI option is available for any required AI activity."

## Designing assignments

- State the learning goal, then decide whether AI helps or undermines it.
- Ask for process evidence: drafts, reflections, short oral explanations, or in-class work.
- Use local, current, or personal-experience prompts that general AI answers handle poorly.
- Have students critique and correct an AI-generated answer.
- Provide an accessible alternative that does not require a paid tool.

## Using Copilot in your own teaching work

- Draft rubrics, practice questions, worked examples, or alternative explanations from published or your own course materials, then review them carefully.
- Check accessibility of anything you generate: headings, alt text, reading level, and color contrast.
- Do not upload student work, grades, or accommodation information to obtain feedback or grades under the general approval. That requires a separate organizational approval.
- AI-detection scores are not proof of misconduct. Follow the organization's academic-integrity process.
