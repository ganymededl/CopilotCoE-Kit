# Prompt library by role
> Ready-to-adapt prompts for faculty, researchers, staff, IT, communications, and leaders. Replace the [brackets] and use only data you are allowed to use.

Copy a prompt, replace every [bracket], and review the result before you use it. Do not paste student records, grades, health, personnel, or other confidential information unless your organization has approved Copilot for that data. Share prompts that work well in the Share Your Prompt channel.

## Faculty: teaching and course design

### Syllabus outline

"I'm designing a course on [topic] for [level]. Draft a 15-week outline with weekly learning objectives and two checkpoints for discussing appropriate AI use."

### Assessment design

"Design 5 formative assessments for [topic] that test reasoning rather than recall, and explain how each one checks understanding."

### Office-hours FAQ

"From these anonymized common questions about [topic], write a one-page FAQ in plain language: [paste questions without names]."

### Syllabus AI statement

"Draft a syllabus section on student use of AI tools that covers permitted uses, required disclosure, academic integrity, and accessibility."

### Feedback on a sample

"Using this instructor-written sample paragraph, suggest 5 specific, actionable feedback comments that push toward deeper analysis: [paste sample]."

### Lesson plan

"Turn this lecture outline on [topic] into a 50-minute lesson plan with timing, speaking notes, and 3 discussion questions."

### Case study

"Write a realistic case study on [topic] set in [context], with background, a dilemma, 4 discussion questions, and the learning objectives it supports."

### Course description editor

"Improve the clarity of this public course description: [paste description]. Preserve all learning outcomes, prerequisites, commitments and assessment rules. List any ambiguities for the instructor instead of resolving them yourself."

### Rubric builder

"Create a 4-level rubric for [synthetic assignment type] that assesses [skill]. Add a row describing how students should disclose any AI assistance. This is a practice draft, not an adopted assessment rubric."

## Research and grants

### Literature map

"Summarize the main debates in [field] since [year] from the sources I attach. List the open questions and note which source supports each point."

### Specific aims

"Draft a one-page specific-aims outline for a proposal on [topic] to [funder]. Use my notes: [paste notes]. Mark every claim that needs a citation."

### Plain-language summary

"Rewrite this abstract for a general audience at an 8th-grade reading level without losing accuracy: [paste abstract]."

### Methods check

"Review this methods section for missing details a reviewer would ask about: [paste section]."

## Administration and student services

### Policy draft

"Draft a first version of a [process] procedure using these requirements: [paste]. Flag anything that needs legal or HR review."

### Process map

"List the steps, hand-offs, and delays in this process description, then suggest three improvements: [paste description]."

### Action list from your notes

"Turn these public or synthetic notes into an action list with owners and dates: [paste notes]. Use only the supplied information. Mark missing owners and dates as not specified; do not invent them."

### Clear communication

"Rewrite this public notice so a new student can understand it in under a minute: [paste notice]. Keep all dates, requirements and commitments unchanged. List any unclear points for me to review."

## Students: study responsibly

Use these only where your course permits AI assistance. Do not supply assessed work or other people's personal information.

### Learn a new topic

"Help me understand [public concept] at [study level]. Explain it in plain language, give an example and ask three questions to check my understanding. Identify claims I should verify in my course materials; do not invent citations."

### Review a study explanation

"Review this explanation I wrote for practice: [paste your own permitted notes]. Identify unclear reasoning and facts I should verify. Ask questions that help me revise it myself; do not write assessed work for me or invent citations."

## Champions: share what works

### Record a reviewed prompt pattern

"Organize these non-sensitive practice notes into a reusable prompt pattern: [paste notes]. Include the task, source limits, prompt, manual corrections, actual time including review, result and limitations. Mark anything not recorded as not measured; do not invent savings or approvals."

## IT and service desk

### Knowledge article

"Using this public Microsoft documentation, write a step-by-step help article for [task] with a short troubleshooting section: [paste or link]."

### Ticket pattern summary

"From these anonymized ticket summaries, group the issues into themes and suggest one knowledge article per theme: [paste without names]."

### Change notice

"Write a short, friendly notice announcing [change] on [date], what users must do, and where to get help."

## Communications and marketing

### Event promotion

"Write three versions of a 50-word announcement for [event] aimed at [audience]: formal, friendly, and social-media."

### Accessibility pass

"Review this text for plain language and accessibility, and suggest alt text for these images: [describe images]."

## Data and analysis (Excel)

### Explain the data

"Describe the trends in this table, the three largest changes, and any values that look like errors."

### Build a formula

"Write an Excel formula that [goal] using columns [A, B, C], and explain how it works."

## Leadership and planning

### Options memo

"Compare these options for [decision] in a table of benefits, risks, cost drivers, and open questions: [paste options]."

### Briefing

"Prepare a one-page briefing on [topic] for [audience] with a recommendation, the evidence, and what we still need to know."

## Make your own

Use the Prompt Ingredients one-pager in the Share Your Prompt channel: **goal** (what you want), **context** (who it is for and why), **source** (which files or data to use), and **expectations** (format, length, tone).
