# Start in 10 minutes
> New to Copilot? Sign in, check that you are protected, try three prompts for your role, and check the result before you use it.

Microsoft Copilot Chat is available to your organization's faculty, staff, and eligible students. These four steps take you from sign-in to a useful first result.

## 1. Sign in with your work or school account (2 minutes)

1. Go to **copilot.cloud.microsoft**, or open the Microsoft Copilot app.
2. Sign in with your **work or school account**, not a personal Microsoft account.
3. Look for the **enterprise data protection indicator** near the prompt box (the exact icon and color are set by Microsoft and can change, so check what the current Copilot app shows). It confirms enterprise data protection applies to this conversation.

**Don't see the indicator?** Stop. Sign out and sign in again with your work or school account. Until you see it, use public information only.

## 2. Know what you can paste (1 minute)

Follow your organization's data-classification rules. The [Governance and Responsible AI](CoE-Governance-and-Responsible-AI.aspx) page shows which data can go into which AI tool.

- **Public information**, such as published pages and announcements: fine with any account.
- **Internal information**, such as planning notes and draft procedures: only with your work or school account and the enterprise data protection indicator showing, and only if your organization's rules allow it.
- **Confidential information**, such as student records, grades, accommodation details, health, personnel, or financial information: don't enter it unless your organization has approved that use.

Not sure? Take the one-minute [Is this OK to use?](Guide-Is-This-OK.aspx) check.

**Something already went wrong?** {{Link:DataSafetyIncident|Report a data or safety incident}}. Stop using the affected content and do not copy or share it. ## 3. Try three prompts (5 minutes)

Pick your role, copy a prompt, and replace the [brackets].

### Faculty
- "Draft a 4-level rubric for a [assignment type] in [course] that assesses [skill]. Add a row describing how students should disclose any AI help."
- "Explain [concept] three ways for first-year students: an analogy, a worked example, and a common misconception to avoid."
- "Write five practice questions on [topic] in order of difficulty, each with the answer and a one-line explanation."

### Staff
- "Rewrite this public notice so a new student can understand it in under a minute. Keep every date and requirement: [paste notice]."
- "Turn these bullet notes I wrote into an action list with owners and dates. Don't add anything I didn't write: [paste your notes]."
- "Draft a polite, 120-word reply declining [request] and suggesting [alternative]." Leave out student names and ID numbers.

### Students
- "Explain [concept] as if I'm new to it, then ask me two questions to check I understood."
- "Quiz me on these notes I wrote, one question at a time, and give hints before answers: [paste your notes]."
- "Give me feedback on the structure of this outline I wrote. Don't rewrite it: [paste outline]." Check your syllabus first: your instructor decides how AI may be used in each course.

### IT and service desk
- "Using this public Microsoft documentation, write a step-by-step help article for [task] with a short troubleshooting section: [paste or link]."
- "Write a short, friendly notice announcing [change] on [date], what users must do, and where to get help."
- "Explain this error message and list the likely causes, most likely first: [paste error]." Remove usernames, IP addresses, and device names first.

## 4. Check before you use it (2 minutes)

- **Verify** facts, numbers, dates, names, and citations. Copilot can be wrong, and a confident tone is not proof.
- **Review** tone, accessibility, and reading level for your audience.
- **Disclose** AI help in material you share, following your organization's guidance.

## Where Copilot shows up

- **On the web** at copilot.cloud.microsoft, and in the **Microsoft Copilot app** on desktop and mobile.
- **Inside Outlook**, if your organization has enabled it. When you use Copilot Chat inside Outlook, it can read the email you have open and may use your inbox and calendar. Don't ask Copilot about confidential email. (Copilot Chat does not add this in-app experience inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, or OneNote - that embedded authoring experience is part of the licensed Microsoft Copilot product; see [Who gets what](Guide-Who-Gets-What.aspx). Microsoft's own documentation notes that exact in-app availability varies by tenant configuration and rollout status, so confirm what your organization currently sees before relying on this.)
