# Microsoft Copilot: starter FAQs

## What is Copilot, and why did the name change?

Microsoft 365 Copilot is now called **Microsoft Copilot**, and Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat is now called **Microsoft Copilot Chat**. The main web address is copilot.cloud.microsoft. Older names still appear in some apps and training materials while the change rolls out. See [Manage Microsoft Copilot Chat](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/manage).

## Which Copilot do I have?

It depends on your Microsoft 365 plan and whether your organization has assigned you a paid Copilot license. The label in the Copilot app or in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint shows **Copilot Chat (Basic)**, **Copilot (Basic)**, or **Copilot (Premium)**. Only Premium searches across all your work or school email, files, chats, and meetings. Copilot Chat works with the file or email you have open in a Microsoft 365 app, and in Outlook it may also use your inbox and calendar. See the **Who gets what** guide on this site.

## Can students use Copilot?

Students 13 and older who sign in with their school account can use Copilot Chat at no extra cost on Microsoft 365 A1, A3, or A5 education plans. Each instructor decides whether and how AI may be used in a course. Students should check the syllabus and ask when it is unclear. See the **Students and AI** guide.

## Does Copilot replace my judgment?

No. Copilot can be incomplete, wrong, or out of date. Check important facts against a reliable source, review everything before you share it, and remain responsible for the final work and any decision.

## Can I put any information into a prompt?

No. Follow organizational data-classification, privacy, records, and acceptable-use rules. Do not enter student records, grades, accommodation details, personnel matters, health or financial information, passwords, or confidential research unless that specific use has been approved and the Copilot service is configured for it.

## Can Copilot see everything in Microsoft 365?

No. Premium Copilot can only use files, mail, and chats that you can already open, and Copilot Chat only uses what you give it, the file or email you have open, and (in Outlook) your own inbox and calendar. That is exactly why overshared SharePoint sites and Teams files matter: if you can open something you should not, Copilot can find it too. Report it rather than use it.

## Does a response always include sources?

Not always. When citations appear, open them and check that they say what Copilot claims. A citation or confident tone is not proof.

## How should I write a useful prompt?

Say what you want, give the context and audience, name the source to use, and describe the format. For example: "Using the attached published course catalog page, write a 150-word plain-language summary for new students." Review the answer and refine.

## What are agents?

Agents are Copilot helpers set up for a specific job, such as Study and Learn, Researcher (paid license), or a department FAQ agent. Use agents from trusted sources. Do not build or share an agent that uses student or personnel data without approval. See the **Using and building agents** guide.

## Can I use Copilot for grading, admissions, hiring, advising, or discipline?

Not as the basis for a decision. These uses affect people and have legal, fairness, privacy, accessibility, and records obligations. They need the organization's formal approval and a qualified person must make and own the decision.

## What should I do if an answer seems wrong, harmful, or shows me something I should not see?

Stop using the affected content and do not copy or share it. {{Link:DataSafetyIncident|Report a data or safety incident}}. ## Who can help me?

Start with your department champion for everyday questions. Use the {{Link:ServiceDesk|IT service desk}} for access problems or technical issues, and the separate incident route above for data or safety concerns.
