# Proposed {{OrgNoun}}-wide Artificial Intelligence Governance Policy

**ILLUSTRATIVE DRAFT FOR {{OrgNameUpper}} REVIEW — NOT {{OrgNameUpper}} POLICY**

Not adopted policy. Not evidence of an organizational decision, appointment, purchase, or completed activity. This ready-to-edit sample proposes institutional provisions for {{OrgShort}} adaptation and authorized organizational review. It has no effective date. References to current organizational policies are reconciliation points, not claims that their exact contents were researched.

## 1. Institutional purpose

The organization will govern artificial intelligence to support learning, accessible services, and responsible operations while preserving human accountability and public trust. AI will be treated as a capability with educational, social, information, and operational consequences, not merely as a software entitlement.

The organization will favor uses that help people prepare, explain, and improve work without delegating judgments about individuals to a model. Educational value will include whether students can understand an activity, obtain appropriate help, and participate without an avoidable technology barrier. Operational value will include quality and review effort, not only the speed of producing a first draft.

All of the organization’s schools, campuses, and online programs are in scope for this proposed policy. No participation requirement will assume equal access to devices, connectivity, paid subscriptions, or assistive technology compatibility.

## 2. Scope and relationship to other obligations

This policy will cover organization-provided AI, purchased or developed systems, embedded features, agents, connectors, and personal services used for organizational work. It will apply to faculty, staff, contractors, and others acting for the organization. Student academic use will remain subject to adopted academic requirements and course-specific faculty rules.

This policy will supplement, not replace, applicable privacy, security, accessibility, records, procurement, employment, research, academic-integrity, and intellectual-property requirements. Where obligations conflict or authority is unclear, the use will remain on hold while the responsible functions resolve the question. Neither a license nor a successful pilot will override another obligation.

## 3. Accountability and decision rights

The sponsoring academic or business function will define the problem, affected people, intended outcome, alternatives, and human oversight. It will remain accountable for the service or work product. The service owner will maintain the approved configuration, source information, access controls, support arrangements, and lifecycle record.

The proposed Center of Excellence coordinating function will maintain the inventory, route reviews, support staff learning, and track conditions. It will not become the authority for every issue it coordinates. Academic governance will retain institution-wide academic decisions; faculty will retain course rules within adopted requirements. Information technology and security will determine technical readiness and safeguards. Stewards will determine authorized data use with privacy and records functions. Procurement and budget functions will retain contracting and spending responsibilities.

Executive authority will resolve institutional priorities and accept residual risk only within lawful delegation. Required determinations cannot be replaced by a steering majority. No role described here represents an appointment or delegation already made.

## 4. Authorized assistance and professional responsibility

Staff may use an explicitly authorized service within its documented purpose and data boundary. Suitable baseline examples include drafting explanations from verified public sources, preparing faculty-reviewed practice materials, and improving a routine nonsensitive process description. Users must minimize input and verify output before relying on it.

Verification will include factual accuracy, source validity, calculations, audience suitability, accessibility, and unintended bias. Generated citations must be checked; confident wording is not evidence. A qualified person must have sufficient time and source information to correct or reject output. Staff must not fabricate approvals, references, appointments, records, or completed work.

AI assistance will not replace official records or required professional judgment. Material assistance must be disclosed where course rules, publication requirements, law, or the nature of the interaction requires it. Public-facing AI services must clearly identify their nature and provide a practical route to human assistance.

## 5. Data authorization and minimization

Public information may be used only where it was intentionally released by an authorized source and the purpose remains appropriate. Publicly available personal information must not be assembled to profile students or employees. Copyright and source accuracy remain relevant even for public material.

Internal information may be processed only after the relevant steward confirms that it is nonsensitive for the proposed task and the service and configuration are approved. A routine planning outline could qualify; a document containing student details, personnel concerns, or confidential operational information would not qualify merely because it is stored internally.

Restricted information has no baseline AI approval. FERPA-sensitive educational records, identifiable advising notes, grades, accommodation information, financial-aid data, personnel records, confidential legal material, credentials, and security-sensitive content require a separate protected-data determination. Mixed content will receive the most protective applicable handling.

Removing names does not automatically de-identify information. Reviewers must consider free text, small cohorts, unusual circumstances, and combinations of attributes. Staff uncertain about classification must pause and consult the relevant steward rather than choose the more convenient category.

## 6. Service boundaries and Microsoft Copilot

Approval of Microsoft Copilot will not authorize consumer AI products, browser extensions, third-party agents, connectors, or separate services. Product identity, configuration, data flows, and terms must be evaluated for the actual use.

Grounded responses can draw on information available through a user’s existing permissions. Those permissions are not, by themselves, authorization to process every accessible item for an AI task. Service owners must address oversharing and source permissions before expansion, and users must follow purpose limitations even where a service can retrieve more information.

Reviewers must examine processing commitments, applicable subprocessors, connected experiences, retention and deletion, and external integrations. The organization will not rely on a universal “data never leaves the tenant” guarantee. Microsoft’s official privacy documentation describes specified protections and commitments; it does not replace organizational data authorization or assessment of a particular configuration.

## 7. Academic freedom, course expectations, and student access

Academic functions will establish institution-wide principles through authorized processes. Faculty will explain course expectations for AI assistance, attribution, collaboration, and assessment. This policy will neither grant a universal right to submit generated work nor prohibit all student learning assistance.

Required activities will offer an accessible, educationally equivalent route that does not depend on purchasing an unapproved subscription. Faculty must assess whether a tool meaningfully supports the intended learning rather than allowing convenience to determine pedagogy. Generated feedback and practice materials will remain drafts requiring subject review.

Student work must not be uploaded for grading, feedback, or detection under a public-content authorization. AI detector scores and generated suspicion must not serve as proof of misconduct. Academic concerns will follow established evidence and review processes, preserving opportunities for students to explain their work.

## 8. Student services and consequential decisions

The organization will distinguish general information from individualized guidance. A public registration explainer may help students find their next step; a generated course recommendation based on an individual record raises different obligations. Staff must verify deadlines, eligibility statements, and referral information before communicating them.

Admissions, individual advising, grading, financial aid, discipline, and employment affect rights or opportunities and fall outside baseline assistance approval. AI output must never be the sole basis for a consequential decision. A nominal human approval is insufficient where the reviewer lacks expertise, time, evidence, or freedom to disagree.

Any proposed assistance in a consequential workflow must undergo enhanced review of lawful purpose, authority, validation, bias, accessibility, explanation, correction, appeal, and meaningful human oversight. Approval of assistance will not authorize automated final decisions. The organization will refuse workflows that cannot meet required protections.

## 9. Prohibited surveillance and profiling

The organization will not use this program to surveil students or create behavioral profiles, including inferences about motivation, vulnerability, misconduct, disability, or likely success from communications and activity. Describing profiling as retention support or program evaluation will not make it permissible.

Staff must not infer protected characteristics or use proxies to allocate opportunities. The organization may evaluate services through separately authorized, proportionate methods, but will not convert security logs or prompt histories into individual performance ratings or student risk scores. Public data and removal of names do not cure an impermissible purpose.

## 10. Review before pilot or release

The sponsor will document purpose, alternatives, users, affected groups, information categories, integrations, decision influence, accessibility, records needs, support, review capacity, and proposed measures before a new use begins. Testing with actual restricted information counts as use and must not precede authorization.

The companion leadership risk policy provides the detailed classification, protected-data gate, and disposition procedure. Low-impact public drafting may follow a formally established standing authorization. Internal-data workflows and integrations require case-specific review. Restricted or consequential uses require enhanced review regardless of pilot size.

Protected-data review must establish lawful basis, permitted purpose, necessary elements, authorized recipients, access controls, contractual conditions, records handling, and incident arrangements. FERPA-sensitive information requires evaluation of applicable legal conditions; product terms or assumed student consent alone will not satisfy that gate.

Conditional authorization will not permit launch until prerequisite controls are verified. Missing evidence will result in hold, redesign, or refusal, not approval by silence.

## 11. Accessibility, equity, and participation

Accessibility review will consider the actual interface, documents produced, user support, and alternatives. Vendor statements will inform but not replace evaluation. AI-generated captions, translations, plain-language text, and alternative descriptions must be reviewed for accuracy and suitability.

Sponsors will consider whether the use adds burdens for people with assistive technology, varied language needs, limited connectivity, or different schedules. Feedback routes must be accessible and must not pressure people to disclose disability or sensitive circumstances. A benefit to the majority will not excuse a serious unresolved barrier for a smaller group.

## 12. Purchasing, resources, and exit

Procurement will review trials as well as purchases when organizational data or obligations are involved. Review will include terms, data processing, security assurances, accessibility, training commitments, intellectual property, breach obligations, records handling, export, deletion, and exit arrangements.

Allocation will consider educational and service value, readiness, equitable reach, and total support costs. A license will not be an award based on title or enthusiasm. Sponsors must account for verification and support labor. Renewals must examine actual evidence rather than adoption counts alone.

The service owner will establish a workable exit path, preserve required records under applicable schedules, remove unnecessary access, and stop unauthorized processing when a capability is retired. This policy supplies no dollar amount, license quantity, or retention period.

## 13. Measurement and public claims

Each pilot will define its baseline, proposed target, comparison method, and actual outcome separately. An unmeasured result will be labeled “not yet measured.” License activation, attendance, and prompt volume are activities, not demonstrated learning or productivity gains.

Time comparisons will include source verification, corrections, accessibility remediation, and final review. Sponsors must report errors, adverse effects, and limitations alongside benefits. Aggregate reports must minimize personal information and suppress details that could expose individuals in small groups.

The organization will not infer student success or employee effectiveness from tool usage. Claims about improved learning require an appropriately authorized evaluation, not a demonstration of generated content. Preliminary estimates must remain estimates in executive summaries.

## 14. Records, incidents, and change management

Records management will determine which prompts, outputs, approvals, and review evidence constitute organizational records and how applicable schedules govern them. Users must not assume that chat deletion satisfies institutional requirements or that all prompts should be copied into a central repository.

Staff will report suspected disclosure, unauthorized processing, material inaccuracies, or harmful behavior through established incident and supervisory routes. They must contain further exposure where safe and preserve necessary evidence in authorized storage without spreading restricted data through ordinary feedback channels.

Service owners must reassess authorization when purpose, users, information, models, integrations, configuration, terms, or decision influence changes. Material control failures will pause the affected workflow. Corrective actions must be verified before restoration or expansion.

## 15. Training, exceptions, and institutional review

Training will cover service boundaries, classification, verification, course expectations, accessibility, disclosure, and reporting. Supervisors will ensure that staff understand the limits relevant to their work. Completion will not grant independent authority to approve new uses.

Exceptions may address a provision only where the responsible authority has lawful discretion. They must specify necessity, scope, alternatives, compensating controls, oversight, and expiry or an event-based end. Exceptions cannot waive law, required contractual obligations, prohibited profiling, or the ban on sole-basis consequential decisions.

This draft proposes monthly coordinated reviews, quarterly portfolio evaluation, and annual policy reassessment after adoption. These are proposed operating provisions, not descriptions of existing committees or completed activities. Final reconciliation must establish lawful delegations and align the text with current organizational requirements through authorized processes.
